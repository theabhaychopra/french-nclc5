The exam

What the TCF Canada is, what score you need, and how to book and sit it. Fees and dates are set by each test centre, so always confirm on the centre's site.

At a glance

1 · Listening35 min39 multiple-choice questions. Audio plays once. Questions get harder as you go.
2 · Reading60 min39 multiple-choice questions on short texts: signs, emails, articles. Harder as you go.
3 · Writing60 min3 tasks: a short message, a story or experience, and an argument comparing two opinions.
4 · Speaking12 minFace to face with an examiner, 3 tasks: an interview, a role-play where you ask the questions, and an opinion.

About 2 h 47 in total, usually in one day. Most centres run listening, reading and writing on a computer.

What each task wants

Writing 1 · Message (60–120 words). Invite, ask, complain or explain to a friend, neighbour or colleague. Greeting, purpose, 2–3 details, closing.
Writing 2 · Experience (120–150 words). Tell what happened (passé composé), describe it (imparfait), give your opinion.
Writing 3 · Argument (120–180 words). Two short documents disagree. Summarise both (40–60 words), then give your view with reasons (80–120 words).
Speaking 1 · Interview (2 min). The examiner asks about you: work, city, hobbies, plans. Your memorised self-intro covers most of it.
Speaking 2 · Role-play (5 min 30, incl. 2 min prep). You get a situation and you ask the questions. Ask lots of varied questions.
Speaking 3 · Opinion (4 min 30). A question like "Is remote work good?" Give your view, two reasons, an example and a counterpoint.

How to book (Hamilton area)

  1. Pick a centre. Alliance Française de Toronto runs the TCF Canada at Spadina, North York, Mississauga and Oakville. Oakville and Mississauga are the closest to Hamilton. Alliance Française d'Ottawa also runs it.
  2. Watch for registration to open. At Alliance Française de Toronto, a session's registration usually opens about a month before the test, and seats are limited. Check their exams page now so you know your target session.
  3. Register and pay online. About CAD $390 at Alliance Française de Toronto. Fees are generally not refundable, and changing your date costs extra.
  4. Register with the ID you will bring. Use your passport, with your name exactly as it appears on it.
  5. Wait for the confirmation (the "convocation"), which tells you the time and room.

Test day

  • Bring your original, valid photo ID: the same one you registered with. No photocopies.
  • Arrive 30 minutes early. They check your ID and take your photo, which goes on your certificate.
  • Your phone stays off and put away for the whole test.
  • Bring water and a snack. It is almost 3 hours with breaks.
  • In speaking: speak slowly, use your templates, and if you are stuck say « Comment dire… ? » and keep going.

After the test

  • Results usually arrive in about 2–3 weeks as a PDF certificate (the attestation de résultats).
  • Results are valid for 2 years. IRCC refuses applications with expired results.
  • You can retake it as often as you want, but there is a waiting period between attempts (about 3–4 weeks). Check the current rule with your centre.
  • On IRCC forms, enter your NCLC levels using the table above.
